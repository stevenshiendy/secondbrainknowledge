**Practice material.** Every flashcard and quiz item is built from the chapter it names. The mock exam in `practice.json` copies the mock midterm's format (four 25-mark essays, 120 minutes, closed book, a choice in Question 4) but asks new questions so that it does not duplicate `exam-questions.md`. Its Question 2 sets an invented scenario (a 30-employee Indonesian food-processing firm) so students can apply the Session 5 theories; the scenario is not in the materials.

**exam-questions.md.** At the student's request, the worked answers to the mock midterm use the course materials first and add three free, open-access sources where the slides do not cover a required part. Each outside point is marked **[outside the slides]** in the file. Each question's "Remember it as a line" section (the five stops, the one-breath line and the short words such as born, pushed, noticed) is a memory aid written for the student, not the authors' wording; it adds no facts beyond the model answer:
- Tanev (2012), *Technology Innovation Management Review*, CC BY 3.0: a definition of born-global firms and how they relate to the international new venture label (Question 1 asks for the difference; the slides do not define born globals).
- Hofstede (2011), *Online Readings in Psychology and Culture*, CC BY-NC-ND 3.0: the sixth dimension, indulgence versus restraint (Question 3).
- Bosma and Kelley (2019), GEM 2018/2019 Global Report: listed as the open-access original of the GEM 2018 data the slides quote. Its figures were not re-checked against the report; the answers use the figures as the slides give them.
Scholars named on the slides are cited in APA 7th as secondary sources through Pruthi and Mitra (2023), on the assumption, stated on every reference slide, that the decks follow that textbook. None of this outside material is in `walkthrough.html` or `practice.json`.

## Gaps

- **Session 6 is missing.** The folder holds decks for Sessions 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7. There is no Session 6 deck, and the pack does not cover whatever it taught. The textbook's key topics (Session 1, slide 12) include "motivations and foreign market entry strategies", "international new ventures and born-globals", "digitization" and "international venture capital and private equity finance", none of which has its own deck in the folder; Session 6 may have covered one of these.
- **Born globals are named but not defined.** Session 1 defines international new ventures and lists "international new ventures and born-globals" as a key topic; Session 5 names born globals in the critique of the stages model; Session 7 says migrant entrepreneurs who first enter their home country are more likely to found born-global ventures. No slide defines born globals or explains how they differ from INVs, although Question 1 of the mock midterm asks for that difference. The book says so in an exam tip; `exam-questions.md` fills the gap from Tanev (2012), marked as outside material.
- **Regional clusters.** Question 4A asks for "real examples of regional clusters of entrepreneurship (e.g., Silicon Valley)". The only cluster named in the materials is Silicon Valley (Session 3, slide 30).
- **Lecturer.** No deck prints a lecturer's name. The mock paper's file name ends "Kurniadi", which may be the lecturer, but the name is not printed in the paper itself, so `notebook.json` leaves out `lecturer`.
- **Exam date.** The mock paper leaves the date blank; `notebook.json` has no date.
- **NECI framework conditions.** Session 3 says the NECI is built from twelve framework conditions but does not list them, and the chart titled "GEM's national entrepreneurship context framework conditions" (slide 34) shows NECI scores by economy rather than the conditions. The pack does not name the twelve conditions.
- **Session 4 learning objectives.** Slide 3 is titled "Learning objectives (2 of 2)" and lists only LO4 and LO5; LO1 to LO3 do not appear on any slide.
- **Image-only slides.** The employment chart (Session 2, slide 20), TEA chart (Session 3, slide 7), NECI table (slide 33) and NECI chart (slide 34) are images. The pack describes them in words and redraws only the parts that can be read exactly (the top five NECI scores; the 37/42/51% opportunity shares from slide 9's text). The NECI table shows only the top 19 of the 54 economies it names.

## Conflicts

- **Small business size thresholds.** Session 2 defines a small business as under 500 employees and under $5 million in sales (slide 8), gives the EU line as under 50 employees for an SME and under 250 for a medium-sized enterprise (slide 11), and gives size bands of up to 19 (micro), 20–99 (small) and 100–499 (medium) (slide 12). These are different definitions, not errors; the pack presents each with its source.
- **Small business share of jobs, 2018.** Session 1 (slide 3) says small businesses "created 55% of all jobs and 66% of all net new jobs" in 2018; Session 2 (slide 19) says that in 2018 they "provided 55% of all jobs and 66% of all net new jobs since the 1970s". The pack quotes each slide's wording in its own chapter.
- **Net new jobs.** Session 2 gives 64% of net new jobs for 1993–2011 (Liberto, 2019) and 66% "since the 1970s" (SBA, 2018). These cover different periods; both are kept.
- **Course-outcome labels.** The slides call the outcomes "CILO 1" and "CILO 2"; the mock paper calls the same wording "LO1" and "LO2". Session 4 also uses "LO4" and "LO5" for its own chapter objectives. The pack uses CILO for the course outcomes and explains the paper's labels.
- **Session 7 and CILO 1.** CILO 1 names immigrant, ethnic, returnee and transnational entrepreneurs, which is Session 7's subject, but the Session 7 deck lists only CILO 2. The pack tags Session 7 as the deck does.
- **Institution name.** The slides carry a "BINUS Higher Education" logo; the mock paper gives the faculty as BBS UG / Creativepreneurship. The pack uses "BINUS University", as the other notebooks on the shelf do.
- **Spelling.** The slides mix British spelling (labour, behaviour, neighbourhood, favourable, minimise) with -ize endings (organization, internationalization). The pack follows the same mix: British spelling with -ize endings.

## Updates

None of the materials reports a change, so the book has no Update notes. The following come from outside the materials and are kept out of the book:
- **Hofstede's dimensions.** The slides teach five dimensions (Hofstede, 1991). Hofstede's later work adds a sixth, indulgence versus restraint (Hofstede, 2011). Mentioned only in `exam-questions.md`, marked as outside material.
- **Data age.** The statistics are from 2014–2019 (USBA 2018, GEM 2018, NVCA 2019). GEM has published later global reports, and Facebook has since been renamed Meta. The book keeps the course figures, which are what the exam marks.
- **GEM report year.** The slides cite "Bosma and Kelley (2018)"; the report is the GEM 2018/2019 Global Report, published January 2019. The book keeps "Bosma and Kelley (2018)"; `exam-questions.md` lists it as 2019 in its APA reference list.

## Source problems

- **Chart titled as framework conditions shows scores.** Session 3, slide 34 is titled "GEM's national entrepreneurship context framework conditions" but plots NECI scores by economy; its source line is cut off behind the chart, and Cyprus appears twice on the axis.
- **Income labels in the NECI table.** Session 3, slide 33 labels Indonesia and India "low income". The pack repeats the label as given.
- **Garbled sentences.** Session 3, slide 10: "opportunity entrepreneurship starts to increase between as income levels rise". Slide 19: "Most people … believe successful entrepreneurs have high status than think entrepreneurship is a good career choice". Session 7, slide 7: "their 'dual habitus' in the country of destination of the country of origin". The pack reads them as "increase as income levels rise", "more people believe … than think …" and "in the country of destination and the country of origin".
- **Typos.** "owing" for "owning" (Session 3, slide 6); "Feminity" (slide 22); "friend or friend" for "friend of a friend" (Session 5, slide 13); "knowledge ad experience" (slide 19); "The ideas was based" (Session 4, slide 13). Corrected silently in the book.
- **Unclear example.** Session 5, slide 21 refers to "federal grants from the government in Las Vegas, Nevada"; federal grants come from the national government, not a city. The pack repeats the slide's wording.
- **"Three key aspects" split across slides.** Session 5, slides 16–17 list the three network aspects across two slides under the same heading; the pack lists them together.
- **Attribution of the 50% jobs figure.** Session 1, slide 3 says immigrant-founded start-ups such as Uber and eBay "significantly contribute to economic growth, accounting for as many as 50% of gross jobs created annually between 1980 and 2010 (NVCA, 2019)". It is unclear whether the 50% refers to immigrant-founded start-ups or to start-ups generally. The pack keeps the slide's wording.
- **Expanded abbreviations.** The slides do not spell out NVCA, OECD, CILO or G7. The pack spells them out (National Venture Capital Association; Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development; course intended learning outcome; Group of Seven) as glosses only.
