- `20250703174420_Sesi 13-14.pptx`: Franchise Agreements and Legal Frameworks (18 slides)

Most of the slide text sits in SmartArt diagrams rather than ordinary text boxes; it was read from the diagram data, and every slide was also rendered and checked by eye. The pictures on the Disney and ARM slides are photographs with no extra text. No speaker notes, lecturer name, course outline, exam information, readings or past papers are in the folder.

**Practice material.** No exam format is given anywhere in the materials, so the mock exam is a balanced practice paper designed for this pack (120 minutes, 100 marks: five 10-mark concept questions and four application questions). The instructions say so. Question B2 sets an **invented scenario** (an animation studio licensing a cartoon character in Southeast Asia); its model answer uses only the course's frameworks and the Disney case. All other questions, model answers, quiz items and flashcards restate the slides. Quiz distractors (wrong options) may go beyond the slides, as the brief allows; no `why` does.

**No exam-questions.md.** The lecturer gave no kisi-kisi, review sheet, mock paper or past paper, so there is no Kisi-kisi file.

## Gaps

- **Strategic alliances have no deck of their own.** They are in the course title, in CILO 1 and CILO 2, and the Tjemkes, Vos & Burgers (2023) *Strategic Alliance Management* textbook is listed on every deck, yet the folder's only alliance content is the Sessions 1–2 overview (definition, advantages, challenges, comparison table, Starbucks + Tata). The book says so in its lede and Start here chapter and adds nothing on alliances beyond those slides. If the course has further sessions (for example 15–16) on alliances, they are not in the folder.
- **No course outline**, so it is not possible to check whether any listed session is missing.
- **Videos.** Each sources slide links a YouTube video (Sessions 1–2: fGyqPaG810Q; 3–4: qPwon6F1MEU; 5–6: G2JXLQrTZXI; 7–8 and 13–14: a84HLk25oAk; 9–10: GkCfd3GaDSs; 11–12: a playlist PLiSkB_X_wEZHHwTL7cmDEXA-T9Jvkw0Sd). The videos are not in the folder and were not used.
- **Textbook chapters.** The decks cite Sherman (2011) chapters 1–9 and 11, but the textbook itself is not in the folder; the pack uses only what the slides say.
- **Sessions 13–14 assignment.** It refers to a sample franchise agreement "distributed in class or via LMS", which is not in the folder.
- **Exam.** No exam type, length, date or format appears in the materials, so `notebook.json` has no `exam` field and no lecturer field.

## Conflicts

- **Area development: fast or slow?** Sessions 11–12 slide 5 recommends area development "for rapid growth in targeted regions", while the comparison table on slide 10 rates its speed of expansion "Slower" than master franchising. The book reports both as written; the second is a comparison with master franchising only.
- **Area development: early-stage or advanced markets?** Slide 9 calls area development "common in early-stage international expansion", while slide 13 says "advanced markets may support area developers; emerging ones may need master franchising". The book reports both as written.
- **Licensing investment.** Sessions 1–2 rate licensing as "Low" investment and "Low cost"; Sessions 3–4 say it needs "no capital investment by the licensor". Not a real contradiction; the book keeps each deck's wording.
- **ARM's licensees.** Sessions 3–4 slide 4 names Samsung, slide 13 names Apple, Qualcomm and Samsung, and slide 14 names Apple, Broadcom and MediaTek. The book lists all of them.
- **TRIPS date.** The Sessions 7–8 table dates the TRIPS Agreement 1994, and slide 8 says it became effective in 1995. Consistent (agreed 1994, in force 1995); both dates are kept.
- **Two lists of licensing clauses.** Sessions 3–4 give eight "core clauses" and Sessions 7–8 give six "legal clauses" with partly different names. The book keeps both lists separately in each deck's wording and points from one to the other.

## Updates

All of the following come from outside the materials and are kept out of the book; the book teaches what the slides say.

- **Indonesia's franchise regulation.** The slides cite "Ministry of Trade, Regulation No. 71/2019" (Permendag No. 71 of 2019 on franchise implementation). The government regulation on franchising it sits under, PP No. 42 of 2007, was replaced, according to outside knowledge that should be verified, by PP No. 35 of 2024. Worth checking whether the course has updated the Indonesian row.
- **Apple's Lightning connector**, the slides' example of product licensing, has since been replaced by USB-C on new iPhones (from 2023).
- **ARM Holdings** is today usually styled "Arm" and has been owned by SoftBank since 2016; the slides' name "ARM Holdings (UK)" is kept.
- None of the decks reports a change itself, so the book has no **Update** boxes.

## Source problems

- **WIPO listed as an "agreement".** Sessions 7–8 slide 9 lists WIPO under "Key International Agreements", but the same slide expands it as the World Intellectual Property Organization, which is a body, not an agreement. The book calls the list "key international instruments" in prose and keeps the slide heading in the lists.
- **FDD numbering.** Sessions 13–14 number eight "key sections" of an FDD 1–8. Outside the materials, the US FDD has 23 official items with a different numbering (for example, litigation is Item 3). The book keeps the slides' numbering and labels it as the slides' own.
- **Identical sources slides.** Sessions 7–8 and 13–14 end with the same sources slide (Sherman chapters 5–9 and the same YouTube video), which may be a copy-and-paste.
- **"Two years of legal battles"** (Apple vs. Qualcomm) is the slides' figure; the book does not give start dates the slides do not print.
- Minor typing slips on the slides ("Challanges", "Defenitions", "TEKS BOOKS") are corrected silently.
- **Textbook editions.** The textbook slide prints "04." after Sherman and "03." after Tjemkes et al.; the book reads these as the 4th and 3rd editions.

## Abbreviations the slides do not spell out

The slides use these without expanding them; the glossary gives the standard expansion: BFA (British Franchise Association), DMCA (Digital Millennium Copyright Act), FIFA (Fédération Internationale de Football Association), GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade), IoT (Internet of Things), JV (joint venture), LMS (learning management system), 4G and LTE (fourth generation; Long-Term Evolution), SWOT and TOWS, and CILO (course intended learning outcome). TRIPS, USPTO, EUIPO, WTO, WIPO, IP, FDD, FTC and SEP are expanded on the slides.
