# Sources

The pack was built from the seven lecture decks in the course folder "International Business Environment" (ENPR6302032, BINUS University; lecturer shown only as the initials "SS" on the title slides):

- `20250730100253_Session 1 - Globalization in the COVID Era.pptx` (28 slides)
- `20250730100356_Session 2 - Political Economy of Trade.pptx` (22 slides)
- `20250730100404_Session 3 - The International Flow of Funds and the Exchange Rate.pptx` (34 slides)
- `20250730100413_Session 4 - The Cultural Environment.pptx` (30 slides)
- `20250730100421_Session 5 - Legal and Political Environment of Business.pptx` (23 slides)
- `20250730100427_Session 6 - Ethics, Corruption & ESG Foundations.pptx` (29 slides)
- `20250730100442_Session 7 - Entry Strategies in Global Business.pptx` (29 slides)

Slide text was extracted from every deck, and every slide that holds an exhibit as a picture (Exhibits 1.1–1.4, 2.1, 2.3, 4.1, 4.2, 4.4, 5.2, 5.3, 7.3, 8.1, 8.3) or a multi-column layout was rendered and read by eye. The decks follow Gaspar, *Introduction to Global Business* (3rd edition, Cengage 2023), Chapters 1–8, and cite *Global Business Today* (12th edition) as a second text; neither textbook is in the folder, so nothing was taken from them beyond what the slides reproduce. No speaker notes (other than two short source and timing notes), course outline, exam information, readings or past papers are in the folder.

**Practice material.** No exam format is given anywhere in the materials, so the mock exam is a balanced practice paper designed for this pack (120 minutes, 100 marks: four 10-mark concept questions and three 20-mark application questions). Its instructions say so. Question B2 sets an **invented scenario** (a mid-sized consumer electronics firm entering an emerging market); its model answer uses only the course's frameworks (Exhibit 8.1, the risk–return trade-off and the Session 5 risks). All other questions, model answers, quiz items and flashcards restate the slides. Quiz distractors (wrong options) may go beyond the slides, as the brief allows; no `why` does. The flashcards include an "Abbreviations" deck generated from the glossary, marked not quizzable.

**Easy answers tab.** `easy-answers.html` retells the seven mock-exam answers in easy English, with the logic behind each answer, one figure per question and a memory line. Every fact restates the slides; the memory lines and the "Let Real Values Make Many Artists Even Smarter" phrase for the eight elements of culture are memory aids, and the tab's lede says so. Answers are sized at about 150 words per 10-mark question and 300 per 20-mark question.

**Mock exam recheck (2026-11-09).** All seven model answers were checked against the slide text. Six matched. Question B2's answer listed franchising in the Exhibit 8.1 ranking but never evaluated it, and named the DFC without the bilateral investment treaty condition on Session 5, slide 9; both were added from Session 7, slide 11 and Session 5, slide 9.

**No exam-questions.md.** The lecturer gave no kisi-kisi, review sheet, mock paper or past paper, so there is no Kisi-kisi file.

## Gaps

- **ESG.** Session 6 is titled "Ethics, Corruption & ESG Foundations", but its slides teach Gaspar Chapter 7 ("Corruption and Ethics in Global Business") and contain nothing on ESG apart from the video "How ESG creates value" on the reference slide. The book covers what the slides teach. ESG is listed in the glossary as "environmental, social and governance", a standard expansion the slides do not print.
- **Supporting people hurt by globalization.** The Session 1 topic list includes "the case made to temporarily support those people negatively affected by globalization", but no slide develops it beyond "globalization does create winners and losers". The book mentions the topic without adding content.
- **Exhibit 4.4** (growth in world merchandise trade by region, 2010–2019) is too small on the slide to read reliably, so its numbers are not reproduced.
- **Gannon's Cultural Metaphors and the GLOBE Project** are only named on the Session 4 slides; the GLOBE constructs and scores come from Exhibits 5.2 and 5.3. Gannon gets no further detail because the slides give none.
- **Icebreakers and videos.** The discussion questions and the YouTube videos on each reference slide were not watched or answered; the book reproduces the questions where they frame a topic.

## Conflicts

- **Swapped labels on Session 5 slides (the book does not follow the slides here).** On slide 7 ("National Economic Ideologies") the headings sit over the wrong descriptions: "Communism" is printed over "the government plays a strong role in the economy and may own stakes in certain businesses" and "Socialism" over "the government or state owns and controls all major factors of production and is philosophically an economically classless society". On slide 12 ("The Legal Environment (1 of 7)") "Civil Law" is printed over the precedent-based description and "Common Law" over "a comprehensive listing of legal rules in sets of written codes of law". Glenn chose to use the standard pairings (communism = state owns all factors, classless; socialism = strong government role; civil law = written codes; common law = precedent), so the book, flashcards and quiz follow those. Worth confirming with the lecturer.
- **Exhibit 2.1 vs Exhibit 1.3.** Both reproduce the World Bank PPP table for 2019 but differ: Germany's PPP GNI is 4,913 in Exhibit 2.1 and 4,013 in Exhibit 1.3; China's population is 1,398 million in Exhibit 2.1 and 1,331 million in Exhibit 1.3 (with Exhibit 1.3 adding growth rates and 2030 estimates). The book uses Exhibit 2.1, which carries the fuller notes.
- **Discount and premium (Session 3, slide 18).** The slide defines a discount as selling a currency at a spot rate below the forward rate, and a premium as selling at a spot rate above it. The book copies these definitions as printed; they read the opposite way to the forward premium p = (F − S)/S used on slides 26 and 29, so they are worth checking against the textbook.

## Updates

- No slide reports a change to a rule or figure, so the book has no Update boxes.
- From outside the materials (kept out of the book): the slides' data run to 2019–2021 (FDI to 2019, largest companies and most dangerous countries 2021, CPI scores from a 2021 article). Later figures exist but the book keeps the course's numbers, since the exam marks against them.

## Source problems

- **FCPA date.** Session 6, slide 24 dates the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act "of 1997". The act is generally dated 1977. The book names the FCPA without a year.
- **Exhibit 4.2 units.** The U.S. balance of payments table is headed "in Billions of Dollars", but its values (e.g. a 2020 current account balance of −647,218) are on a scale of millions, and one cell reads "−7,52,3p5540". The book does not reproduce the table's numbers.
- **Exhibit 4.3** prints no unit for the U.S. exports and imports by partner country; the book copies the numbers and says the unit is not given.
- **Typos copied or smoothed.** "Craigshead's" (Session 4, slide 17) is kept as printed. "Invest profitability" (Session 7, slide 26) is written as "invest profitably". "Wiling" (slide 18) and "safe heaven" (Session 3, slide 14) are corrected to "willing" and "safe haven". "An important tariff" in the preferential duty definition (Session 2, slide 17) is read as "an import tariff".
- **WTO "began trading in 1948 under GATT"** (Session 1, slide 17) is kept in the slide's own words; the book says the WTO's history began in 1948 under GATT.
- **Exhibit 1.2 in Exhibit 1.3.** Slide 10 ("Strategic Competitors 2030") repeats the 2050 ranking from Exhibit 1.2 beside the PPP table; the book treats it as a repeat.
