When a company first starts selling abroad or buying raw materials abroad, the compliance work usually lands on someone in the supply chain team who has little or no background in trade regulation. PART A · WEEK 1 · ORGANIZING FOR EXPORT AND IMPORT

How the export/import structure grows

There is no single correct organization chart. The slides give three variables that decide what the structure looks like: company size, trade volume, and which tasks are done in-house versus outsourced.

Division of labour: as volume rises, it becomes cheaper to have specialists who handle one task many times than generalists who handle many tasks once.

The logicThe same personnel can also hold other roles in the company (for example, a purchasing officer who also files import entries). So "the export department" is a set of functions, not necessarily a separate room of people. In a small firm the functions exist even though they all sit with one person.
Exam trap"A large company must have a separate export department" is false as an absolute rule. The slides say structures vary from company to company; separation is a tendency that follows volume, not a legal requirement.
PART A · WEEK 1 · ORGANIZING FOR EXPORT AND IMPORT

Starting an import/export business, and the import cycle

Part of the deck uses an Indian example (an online guide on starting an import/export business). The steps are general; only the agency names are Indian.

Step zero: create the legal identity

Before any trade, the business needs (1) a brand name or legal entity, kept separate from other ongoing businesses to make customs procedures easier, and (2) a government registration code that identifies it as a trader. In the Indian example this is the IEC (Import Export Code), which requires a tax number (PAN (Permanent Account Number)) first.

Indonesian equivalent (added context, not on the slides): the business identity number is the NIB (Nomor Induk Berusaha), issued through the OSS (Online Single Submission) system, and the tax number is the NPWP (Nomor Pokok Wajib Pajak, Taxpayer Identification Number). The ordering logic is the same: tax identity → business identity → trading permissions. The Indian "CHA" (Customs House Agent) corresponds to a licensed customs broker, in Indonesia a PPJK (Pengusaha Pengurusan Jasa Kepabeanan, licensed customs broker).

The 7-step import cycle

PART A · WEEK 1 · ORGANIZING FOR EXPORT AND IMPORT

Export order processing: four flows, one pattern

Bade's textbook breaks export order processing into four stages. Every stage follows the same hub pattern: the export department receives something, sends it out to the departments that hold the needed information, then consolidates and passes it on.

Abbreviations in this figure: L/C Letter of Credit

The logic behind the sequence
  • Quote before order: the quotation (or pro forma invoice) fixes price and terms; the purchase order is then checked against it ("match to quotation"). Without a quote, there is nothing to verify the order against.
  • Finance checks the L/C before shipment: once goods leave, the exporter loses its leverage. Confirming the buyer's letter of credit is opened is how the exporter makes sure it will be paid before giving up control of the goods.
  • Collection documents are prepared at shipment, used at collection: stage ③ creates what stage ④ needs.
PART A · WEEK 1 · ORGANIZING FOR EXPORT AND IMPORT

The import department

For importers or trading companies, the import department usually starts in one of three ways:

Appointed as a local distributor for a foreign manufacturerBuying foreign products with local sales potentialMoving manufacturing abroad and importing what used to be made at home

Because foreign manufacturers often sell FOB (Free On Board) (the buyer takes over cost and risk once goods are loaded at the origin port), the importer itself must understand ocean shipping, insurance and customs. This pulls the importer into working with foreign forwarders, customs brokers, banks, customs authorities and marine insurers.

Compliance starts at the design stage

Abbreviations in this figure: HS Harmonized System · FTA Free Trade Agreement

Why at design stage? The physical make-up of a product (materials, components, where it is assembled) decides its HS (Harmonized System) code, duty rate, FTA (Free Trade Agreement) eligibility and origin. Once the product is designed and built, those facts are fixed. Changing them afterwards is costly or impossible.

Recordkeeping compliance and the penalty formula

Exporters and importers have always had to keep records of their trade transactions, but these obligations are now mandatory by law. Electronic documents (emails, web purchases, online confirmations, e-invoices) make it more important to show that the customer actually accepted the terms, for example by written email confirmation or a required click-through link before the order completes. Agencies have moved to electronic filing, but that does not remove the duty to keep hard-copy records.

If a company fails to produce documents that U.S. Customs and Border Protection requests, the fine depends on intent and is capped by whichever is less:

Type of failureFixed capPercentage capFine applied
IntentionalUS$100,00075% of appraised valuethe lower of the two
Negligent or accidentalUS$10,00040% of appraised valuethe lower of the two
Work it out
The logicThe percentage cap protects small shipments from a fine larger than the goods are worth; the fixed cap limits the fine on very large shipments. Negligence is fined far less than intent because the law punishes the decision to hide more heavily than a careless mistake. Compare: at a value of US$50,000, intent costs US$37,500 while negligence costs US$10,000.

Layers of law: which law governs what

The textbook (U.S. setting) separates two questions: may these goods cross the border? and is this sales contract valid? Different levels of law answer them.

Plus two outer layers named in the chapter title: international law (treaties such as the WTO (World Trade Organization) agreement and trade agreements) and foreign law (the importing country's own rules, which the exporter must also satisfy).

Exam trapA single transaction is governed by several layers at once. Example: an Indonesian exporter selling to a U.S. buyer must comply with Indonesian export rules, the contract terms (governed by whichever contract law the parties chose or that applies), U.S. import rules (foreign law from the exporter's view), and WTO commitments that shaped both countries' rules.

PART B · WEEK 2 · FEDERAL, STATE, INTERNATIONAL AND FOREIGN LAW

Why governments regulate trade at all

The slides give the purpose of export policy in three points, and the technical requirements that follow from them. Read them as goal → instrument pairs:

Policy goal (slide 7)What it implies in practice
A conducive business climateClear, predictable rules and licensing so exporters can plan
Healthy business competitionSame requirements for every exporter of a product; no one skips standards to undercut prices
National interests and international agreementsControl strategic goods, while staying within WTO and trade-agreement commitments

Technical requirements (slide 6) are how goods prove they meet those goals: meet international or buyer standards (labelling, marking, packaging); pass conformity assessment by quality bodies (LPK (Lembaga Penilaian Kesesuaian, Conformity Assessment Body), LS-Pro (Lembaga Sertifikasi Produk, Product Certification Body) product certification bodies, inspection bodies); and meet K3L (security, health, safety, environment).

The logicNotice that K3L (or K3LM with Moral) appears again and again: in technical requirements, in the classification of export goods, and in the import policy objectives. It is the main justification the government uses whenever it restricts trade. If an exam asks "why is product X controlled?", one strong answer is to identify which K3LM interest it protects.
PART B · WEEK 2 · FEDERAL, STATE, INTERNATIONAL AND FOREIGN LAW

What "export" means legally

Definition from the slides: export is the activity of releasing goods from the customs area. The definition is about crossing a legal boundary, not about selling to a foreigner.

Abbreviations in this figure: EEZ Exclusive Economic Zone (Zona Ekonomi Eksklusif, ZEE)

The port is the legally defined place (land plus surrounding waters with set boundaries) where ships dock and goods are loaded, with safety facilities, so it is where the customs release normally happens.

The logicBecause export is defined as a release from the customs area, the customs export declaration (Pemberitahuan Pabean Ekspor, PEB) is the legal proof of when an export happened. Permendag 11/2024 uses exactly this: mineral concentrates "may only be exported until 31 December 2024 ... as proven by the number and registration date of the customs export declaration". The date of the sale contract does not matter; the date of the customs release does.

Why every export sale should be in writing

Sales come in two kinds: isolated transactions (one-off orders) and ongoing transactions (a continuing relationship). For both, the sales agreement is the most important document in the deal. The slides give three reasons it must be written:

1 · Legal enforceability

Under the U.S. Uniform Commercial Code, a sale above US$500 is enforceable by the seller (so it can claim payment) only if it is in writing. There are exceptions, but writing is the safe practice.

2 · Memory fails

"The thinnest ink is better than the brightest memory." Disputes start when parties did not record, or never discussed, an issue.

3 · Checklist and record

A written agreement reminds both sides what to agree on and records what they agreed. Modifications must also be in writing.

Verbal deals still happen (commodity trading by phone, orders taken at trade shows or meetings). Online selling has made formal agreements less common, which raises the risk of misunderstanding. The seller should also check for conflicting terms between its own form and the buyer's form.

Email ordersAn email order and acceptance can satisfy the "in writing" rule, but it usually contains only quantity, price, and maybe a delivery date. That leaves the seller unprotected on everything else. Fix: the seller's acceptance must state expressly that the sale is subject to the seller's standard terms and conditions, and attach them, with the buyer's acknowledgment, so the buyer cannot say it never saw them.

PART E · WEEK 4 · EXPORTING: SALES DOCUMENTATION (BADE CH. 3)

FCPA: why an agent's bribe can be the exporter's crime

The U.S. Foreign Corrupt Practices Act makes it illegal for an exporter's agent to pay money to a foreign government agency, official or political party to obtain or keep business. The exporter violates the law if it knew about it. Knowledge can be inferred from unusual circumstances that should have warned the exporter:

Agent asks for a special discountan unusual allowancean extra commissionpayment to someone other than the agent→exporter is charged with knowledge
The logicExtra money that has no business reason has to go somewhere. The law treats the exporter as knowing because a careful exporter would have asked where. That is why the agreement should include an FCPA compliance clause.

Licence applications: when the buyer need not supply documents

The first step of an application is to work out what documents the buyer must provide. No buyer documentation is needed when:

  • The end recipient is in the listed countries of the Americas (e.g. Canada, Mexico, Brazil, Chile, Colombia, Peru, Panama, Uruguay, Venezuela, Caribbean states).
  • The recipient or ultimate buyer is a foreign government or agency, other than the People's Republic of China.
  • Goods are for temporary exhibition, demonstration or testing.
  • The application is by or for a registered aid agency exporting to a foreign government agency.
  • The goods are controlled for supply shortage reasons; the application is for software or technology; certain encryption goods; or the Special Comprehensive License procedure.
The logicBuyer documents exist to prove the end user and end use. They are waived where that risk is already low: close regional partners, government buyers, goods that will return, or controls that are about domestic supply rather than security.

Terms of purchase: who carries cost, risk and legal duties

Four things must be agreed in every import purchase: passage of title, risk of loss, price, and payment. The buyer should set its purchasing policy in advance rather than accept whatever each seller offers. Abbreviations such as ex-factory, FOB plant, CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight) and landed help standardise obligations, but can also cause confusion.

Abbreviations in this figure: FOB Free On Board · CIF Cost, Insurance and Freight

Bade's advice: to make the seller responsible for its own country's export laws, the buyer should usually not buy ex-factory, but FOB port of export, CIF, or landed. If the seller fails to obtain an export permit, the buyer suffers anyway: lawsuits, failed delivery, lost supply.

FOB and CIF in one line eachFOB (Free On Board, named port of shipment): seller delivers once goods are on board the ship; cost and risk pass to the buyer there. Both are sea-only terms; "FOB plant" on slide 19 is an older American usage, not Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) FOB. When the buyer may prefer to do more itself (slide 23): the seller is inexperienced in international shipping; competitors buy ex-factory, so the buyer must match their cost; the buyer is buying from an affiliate; the buyer already has warehouse-to-warehouse marine insurance under a blanket policy; or the buyer can get cheaper ocean freight or insurance than the seller. Exam-ready reasoning: the chains to remember

1 · Compliance → profit

Specialized staff prevent fines, delays and seizures; that is why a compliance-oriented program is called profitable.

2 · Volume → specialization

One person in a small firm; separate export and import departments as volume and insourcing grow.

3 · Export orders differ

Delivery, payment security, insurance, packing all change, so many departments must give input, so someone must coordinate.

4 · Hub pattern

In quotation, order entry, shipment and collection, the export department collects input, consolidates and passes it on.

5 · Design decides duty

Classification, duty, FTA eligibility, valuation and origin follow from how a product is made, so import compliance starts at design.

6 · Intent decides the fine

Missing records: intentional = lesser of $100k or 75%; negligent = lesser of $10k or 40%.

7 · Federal border, state contract

Crossing the border is national law; the sale contract is state (private) law. Add international and foreign law on top.

8 · Risk decides the permit

Export = release from the customs area. Free → regulated → supervised → prohibited; regulated goods need ET (who), PE (how much), LS (verify the cargo).

9 · Product + volume decide procedure

Component, spare, finished, set, modified, used: these decide documents and foreign customs treatment. Bigger volume → formal agreement, secure payment, agents.

10 · Four tests of export control

What, Who, Why, Where. Fail one and the deal is blocked. Red flags are things that "do not fit" a normal buyer.

11 · Writing protects the seller

UCC (Uniform Commercial Code): sales over US$500 enforceable by the seller only in writing. Email orders need the seller's T&Cs (Terms and Conditions) attached.

12 · Control when the contract forms

Price list "not an offer"; check RFQ (Request for Quotation) fine print; cost sheet before quoting; no agreement until the seller accepts the PO. Conduct accepts all the offer's terms.

13 · Ownership splits distributor and agent

Distributor buys, owns, bears resale risk, sets its own price. Agent only solicits orders. FCPA: the exporter is liable if it knew of bribes.

14 · Delegate work, not liability

Exporter is responsible for the forwarder's errors. Certificate of origin ≠ origin label. DG (Dangerous Goods), precursor and health documents protect carriers and destination countries.

15 · EAR in order

Scope → other agency → de minimis (10%/25%) → ECCN + Country Chart → 10 prohibitions → exception → licence. NLR is declared under penalty of perjury.

16 · Import mirrors export

Sourcing, supplier vetting, labelling, terms of purchase. Buy FOB/CIF/landed to make the seller handle its own export law. Buying commission not dutiable.

ENPR6304032 · Trade terms · Incoterms® 2020

FOB and CIF

Two of the most common trade terms in sea freight, both defined in Incoterms (International Commercial Terms), the rulebook of trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). Both answer the same three questions: where the seller has delivered, who pays for each leg of the journey, and who bears the loss if the goods are damaged on the way. The diagrams below follow one shipment from the seller's factory to the buyer's warehouse.

FOB
Free On Board · named port of shipment

The seller delivers when the goods are loaded on board the vessel nominated by the buyer at the named port of shipment, cleared for export. From that moment the buyer bears all risk and pays all further costs, including ocean freight and insurance.

CIF
Cost, Insurance and Freight · named port of destination

The seller delivers the same way, on board at the port of shipment, but must also pay the freight and buy minimum insurance to the named port of destination. Risk still passes to the buyer at loading, even though the seller has paid for the voyage.

The journey both terms share

Every sea shipment passes the same nine stages. A trade term simply draws lines on this journey: one line for cost (who pays) and one for risk (who loses if something goes wrong).

The logicFOB is the term for a buyer who wants to control the voyage: it chooses the shipping line, negotiates freight, and decides whether and how much to insure. Write it as CIF [named port of destination] Incoterms® 2020, for example CIF Rotterdam. The named place is in the buyer's country, but the seller still delivers in its own country.

SellerBuyerTwo critical points* unloading is the seller's cost only if its freight contract includes it
The CIF trapCIF names the destination port, so students often think the seller is responsible until the goods arrive. It is not. CIF has two critical points: risk passes at loading in the seller's country (point 1), while costs run to the destination port (point 2). If the ship sinks mid-ocean, the loss is the buyer's. The buyer is protected only because the seller had to buy insurance for the buyer's benefit.

How the price is built: from FOB to CIF

A quotation is only comparable if you know which costs it includes. This is why Week 4 insists on a cost sheet before quoting. Change the numbers to see how the price ladder moves.

Insurance is charged on 110% of the CIF price, which itself includes the premium, so CIF = (FOB + freight) ÷ (1 − 1.1 × rate).

Why this matters in Indonesia (context, not on the slides): Indonesian export values (PEB (Pemberitahuan Ekspor Barang, export declaration)) are recorded on an FOB basis, while import duty is calculated on the customs value at CIF. So the same shipment of US$10,650 FOB pays duty on about US$11,890 when imported. A 10% duty would be about US$1,189, not US$1,065.

Test the logic: four what-ifs

Crane drops the container while loading

If it falls before the goods are on board: seller's loss under both FOB and CIF. Delivery is not yet complete.

Ship sinks mid-ocean

Buyer's loss under both. Under FOB the buyer recovers only if it bought its own insurance. Under CIF it claims on the policy the seller bought.

Freight rates double after the contract

FOB: the buyer pays the higher rate. CIF: the seller absorbs it, because the CIF price was fixed including freight.

Seller cannot get an export permit

Seller's problem under both, because export clearance is the seller's task. This is why Week 7 advises buying FOB port of export or CIF rather than ex-factory.

Exam traps and course links

Sea onlyFOB and CIF are for sea and inland waterway transport only. For containers handed to a carrier at a container terminal, or for air and road, Incoterms recommends FCA (Free Carrier) (instead of FOB) and CIP (Carriage and Insurance Paid To) (instead of CIF), because the seller loses control of the container before it is loaded on the ship. Under CIP the minimum insurance is the wider ICC (A).
"FOB plant" in Week 7 is not Incoterms FOBBade (deck 07, slide 19) lists "ex-factory, FOB plant, CIF, landed" and warns these abbreviations "can also be sources of confusion". "FOB plant" is an older American usage meaning delivery at the seller's factory, close to ex-works. Under Incoterms, FOB always names a port. Always write the full term: FOB [port] Incoterms® 2020.
What Incoterms do not coverWeek 7 says every purchase must settle title, risk of loss, price and payment. Incoterms deal with delivery, risk and costs only. They do not transfer ownership (title) or set the payment method. Those must be written into the sales contract (Week 4).
Version note: the slides do not name an Incoterms edition. Bade (2015) was written under Incoterms 2010. FOB and CIF are the same in substance in 2010 and 2020, including CIF's ICC (C) minimum. The 2020 change raised the minimum insurance for CIP only.

Abbreviations on this page

Each abbreviation written out in full. The full study-pack glossary is at the end of Export-Import Foundations and in the Glossary tab of Export-Import Practice.

PEBPemberitahuan Ekspor Barang
Export goods declaration filed with Indonesian customs. The regulation calls it the customs export declaration (Pemberitahuan Pabean Ekspor); its registration date proves when an export happened.
PIBPemberitahuan Impor Barang
Import goods declaration filed with Indonesian customs; import duty is assessed on it.
IncotermsInternational Commercial Terms
Standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC); current edition Incoterms 2020.
EXWEx Works
Incoterm: seller makes goods available at its own premises; buyer does everything else, even export clearance. Bade's "ex-factory".
FCAFree Carrier
Incoterm: seller delivers to the buyer's carrier at a named place, export-cleared. Used instead of FOB for containers, air and road.
FOBFree On Board
Incoterm (sea only): seller delivers when goods are on board the vessel at the named port of shipment; cost and risk pass to the buyer there.
CIFCost, Insurance and Freight
Incoterm (sea only): seller delivers on board at the port of shipment (risk passes there) but pays freight and minimum insurance to the named port of destination.
CIPCarriage and Insurance Paid To
Incoterm: like CIF but for any transport mode; minimum insurance ICC (A).
ICCInternational Chamber of Commerce · Institute Cargo Clauses
Two meanings. (1) The International Chamber of Commerce publishes Incoterms. (2) ICC (A), (B), (C) are the Institute Cargo Clauses, standard marine cargo insurance covers: (A) is widest ("all risks"), (C) is narrowest.
