Bridging the e-SKA and the Dangerous Goods Declaration so a shipment avoids the Red Channel

The case question

How can exporters bridge the naming difference between the Ministry of Trade's e-SKA (based on HS Code) and the DGD (based on transport safety rules) so it does not trigger a Red Channel at Customs, and what solution do Indonesian regulations support?

1. The problem

  • e-SKA: a trade document proving Indonesian origin for tariff preferences. Goods must be described according to the invoice and HS Code.
  • DGD: a transport safety document. Goods must be described by Proper Shipping Name (PSN) and UN Number.
  • Conflict: the same product has two official names, for example "Solvent-based wall paint, HS 3208" on the e-SKA and "PAINT, UN1263, Class 3" on the DGD. When data is synchronized in INSW, this looks like a mismatch and the documents are held.

2. Legal basis

  • Certificate of Origin rules: Minister of Trade Regulation No. 24 of 2018, as last amended by No. 39 of 2020, governs SKA issuance. Goods descriptions follow the invoice and HS Code. Note: Permendag No. 24 of 2021 regulates distributor and agent agreements, not e-SKA or HS Codes.
  • Law No. 17 of 2008 on Shipping, Articles 44 to 48: dangerous goods must be transported according to regulations, classified by class (e.g. Class 3 flammable liquids), packed and labelled according to the IMDG Code, and reported to the Harbor Master.
  • Minister of Transportation Regulation PM 90 of 2013 (air): packages must be marked with the PSN and UN Number (point 6.3), and the shipper's declaration must be signed by licensed personnel and written in English and Indonesian (points 7.1 and 7.2).
Update since submission

The course study pack (Part J of the walkthrough) records two later changes to the rules cited above:

  • Permendag 24/2018 is still the base rule for issuing the SKA, but it was amended again after 39/2020: Permendag 34/2023 is its fourth amendment. See The Certificate of Origin: Permendag 24/2018 and 39/2020.
  • PM 90/2013 was revoked and replaced by PM 32/2022 on 12 October 2022. The shipper's duties continue under the new number. See UU 17/2008 on Shipping and PM 90/2013 on air cargo.

Both naming standards are legally required, so neither can be replaced by the other.

3. Recommended solution

  1. Keep both names. The e-SKA uses the trade description and HS Code. The DGD uses the PSN and UN Number.
  2. Use the invoice and packing list as the bridge. Write both identities in one line, e.g. "Solvent-based wall paint, HS 3208.20, PAINT, UN1263, Class 3, PG II".
  3. Keep the HS Code consistent across the invoice, export declaration (PEB), and e-SKA, along with matching quantities and weights.
  4. Cross-reference documents by listing the same invoice and B/L or AWB numbers on the DGD.
  5. Attach the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) showing both the trade name and the PSN/UN Number.

4. Supporting case: risk of non-compliance

The Saintara article (Fakhrurrozi & Widodo, 2022) shows how strictly dangerous goods documentation is enforced.

  • Permit procedure: the shipping agent applies to the Head of KSOP with a B/L copy, origin-of-goods letter, manifest, stowage plan, and crew list. The LALA section checks and approves it, and KPLP officers supervise the operation. Each permit covers one activity.
  • Law: Article 216(1) of Law 17/2008 requires Harbor Master approval for handling dangerous goods. Under Article 322, a ship's captain who violates this faces up to 6 months in prison or a fine of up to Rp100 million.
  • Court ruling No. 159/Pid.Sus/2020/PN Tgg: the captain was fined Rp10,000,000 (or 3 months' confinement if unpaid) plus Rp2,000 in court costs.

Errors in dangerous goods documents can lead to held shipments and legal sanctions, so each document must be correct for its own purpose.

Conclusion

The best solution is not to force the e-SKA and DGD to use the same product name. The e-SKA keeps the trade description and HS Code under the Ministry of Trade's origin rules, while the DGD keeps the PSN and UN Number under Law No. 17 of 2008 and PM 90 of 2013. The two are linked through an invoice and packing list showing both identities, consistent HS Codes, cross-referenced document numbers, and an SDS.

This shows Customs that the difference comes from each document's function, not from any inconsistency in the goods, which reduces the risk of document holds and further inspection as long as all information is accurate and verifiable.
